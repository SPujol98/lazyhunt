## manual_analyses
exactly like the candidate's own manual analyses of previous offers

## experience_rule
Required years of experience up to and including ~3 years (e.g. '2-3 años', '3+ years') is a MINOR penalty ONLY - it is NEVER a wall and NEVER by itself a reason to discard: junior candidates normally apply to offers asking for up to 3 years. Years required well above 3 (4+ years) IS a hard wall.

## degree_rule
A degree requirement phrased with ANY equivalence wording ('o similar', 'or equivalent (experience/qualification)', 'Formación en X o similar') ACCEPTS the candidate's higher vocational degree - it is NOT a wall. A degree requirement with NO such equivalence wording IS a hard wall.

## lacking_core_rule
Using AI coding tools in the role's daily workflow is a SMALL PLUS ONLY - it can NEVER outweigh a daily core the candidate lacks (full-stack development with frontend frameworks, cloud-platform engineering, Java/.NET, or BI/data-analyst work). A role whose daily core is full-stack frontend or cloud infrastructure STAYS LOW-SCORED even when it praises AI-assisted development - that praise is never a substitute for the missing core.

## hard_walls
A hard wall is ONLY one of these four cases - never any other requirement, however strict it sounds:
1. A degree requirement with NO equivalence wording (see above).
2. Years of required experience of 4+ (see above; up to and including ~3 years is a minor penalty only, never a wall).
3. Seniority or technical leadership of a team (Senior/Lead/Principal, 'technical reference', managing or mentoring team members).
4. A mandatory stack with ZERO evidence in the claims that is the role's DAILY CORE - what most of the working week is spent on (e.g. Java/Spring, .NET, cloud-platform engineering as the core job, BI reporting, training or fine-tuning your own models as the main job). A generic 'develop and deploy ML models' line, a framework listed as one option among several, or a cloud platform mentioned in the requirements do NOT by themselves make it the daily core.

## soft_walls
EVERYTHING ELSE is a SOFT wall (lower the score a bit, NEVER force discard by itself) - this explicitly includes: a specific tool the candidate lacks that is merely mentioned or listed as a plus; a cloud platform even when stated as a requirement, as long as it is not the role's daily core; a specific framework listed as one option among several; a new business domain the candidate has not worked in before; fluent Catalan when the candidate only has a basic level; and anything phrased as 'nice to have' / 'a plus'.

## positive_signals
Positive signals: junior-friendly wording, vocational training or a bootcamp explicitly accepted, personal projects counted as experience, RAG/LLM/retrieval evaluation or Python backend (FastAPI, PostgreSQL) as the role's core (the candidate's strongest area), data pipelines for logistics or operations, few applicants or a fresh posting.
